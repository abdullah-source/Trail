00:12Problem

01 The problem

A professor flags your essay for AI.
You wrote it. You have nothing to show.

What changed

Colleges now run AI detectors on student work. The detectors are wrong often enough that every campus has a story, and a 2023 Stanford study found they flagged most essays written by non-native English speakers.

Who pays

The student. "I wrote it" is not evidence. The burden of proof has quietly moved onto them, with no tool on their side of the table.

Why now

Detectors arrived faster than due process, and AI-use declarations are becoming standard in syllabi. Students need a record of their own work, and nobody has built one.

00:47Product

03 The product

A Chrome extension that records how you write, on your own laptop.

Cities and silence · Google Docs0 words
typed 0%pasted 0%
  • Replay any essay like a video. Scrub to the paragraph you rewrote four times.
  • Every paste is kept with the site it came from. jstor.org is a quotation; chatgpt.com is a conversation to have honestly.
  • The AI-use declaration your course requires is drafted from what actually happened, not from memory.
  • If you are ever accused, export an evidence pack anyone can verify in a browser without trusting us.
  • Trail does not detect AI. It records process, and it says so on every page.

01:40Retention

06 Useful every week, not just in a crisis

The evidence pack is one item on a menu. The habit is the product.

Replay

Watch 2,000 words happen

The essay at forty seconds a session. Most tools keep the final version; Trail keeps the whole thing.

Patterns

Your process, in numbers

Best hour, median session, typed share, how much you rewrite. Nothing is sent anywhere.

Time

Screen time for homework

Active minutes by class and assignment on Canvas and friends, a streak, and plain habit lines. Student-only. No teacher view, no limits.

Declaration

Written from what happened

The AI-use statement more courses require, drafted from the record. You edit it, you sign it.

Pack

If anyone ever asks

One click. Verifiable by anyone, in a browser, without trusting Trail.

Privacy

Nothing leaves the laptop

The server holds email, fingerprints and signatures. Deleting the account deletes all of it.

07 Customers and how we get them

Start with the students who are most afraid, and let the product carry itself.

Wedge

International students at three campuses

They are flagged most, they talk to each other most, and Claremont is one of the three.

Loop 1

Invite three, get a semester

An invite counts when the friend finishes a first replay, not when they sign up. Nine invites, three free semesters.

Loop 2

The verify page is the ad

Every pack a student hands a professor points to trail.app/verify. The person checking it is the next person recommending it.

Loop 3

The declaration travels

A declaration "generated from my writing record" at the bottom of an essay is read by every grader. Writing centres ask where it came from.

Channel

Chrome Web Store, one click

Five dollars once, no per-install cost. "Add to Chrome" is the whole funnel; the first replay is the activation.

Cost

Acquisition cost near zero

No ads. Students sell it to students, and the free early-access period is the entire marketing budget.

08 Business model

Sold only to students. Never to institutions.

Semester plan$12Every four months. One coffee a month.
Monthly$3.99Same features, month to month.
Server cost per user<2¢Per month at current scale. The server stores fingerprints, not text. Estimate from our Railway bill.
  • Free during early access. Fourteen days free with every feature once pricing switches on, no card.
  • Recording, local storage, export and checkpoint signing never lock, paid or not. Holding the record hostage would make the whole thing pointless.
  • Refusing institutional contracts is the moat. The moment a university is the customer, Trail becomes surveillance and students stop installing it.
  • Cancel in one click, full refund within fourteen days, renewal reminders seven days before. The rules we would want as students.
